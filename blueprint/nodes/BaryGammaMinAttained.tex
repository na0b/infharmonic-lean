\begin{lemma}[Continuity of the slope functional and attainment of its infimum]
Let $F$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, let $I\subseteq\R$, let
$\Omega=\{(y,\sigma):\sigma\in I\}$, and let $f$ be a real function on space-time
continuous on $\Omega$. Let $S\subseteq F\times\R\times\R$ be a nonempty compact
set of triples $(x,t,s)$ with $s>0$ and $t\in I$, and for such a triple write
\[
  g(y)=f(y,t)-\frac{|y|^2}{2s},
  \qquad
  \mathcal G(A;x,t,s)=\bigl\{g(y)-A\cdot(y-x):y\in F\bigr\},
  \qquad
  \Gamma(A;x,t,s)=\sup\mathcal G(A;x,t,s).
\]
Let $\Lambda_0\ge0$ and $M>0$ and assume that for every $(x,t,s)\in S$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\mathcal G(A;x,t,s)$ is bounded above for every $A\in F$;
\item[(ii)] every $A\in F$ with $\Gamma(A;x,t,s)\le\Gamma(0;x,t,s)+1$ satisfies
$|A|\le\Lambda_0$;
\item[(iii)] $\Gamma(A;x,t,s)=\sup_{|y|\le M}\{g(y)-A\cdot(y-x)\}$ for every
$A\in F$ with $|A|\le\Lambda_0+1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $(A,x,t,s)\mapsto\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$ is continuous on
$\{|A|\le\Lambda_0+1\}\times S$, and there is a function
$G:F\times\R\times\R\to\R$, continuous on $S$, such that for every
$(x,t,s)\in S$
\[
  G(x,t,s)\le\Gamma(A;x,t,s)\quad(A\in F),
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  G(x,t,s)=\Gamma(A_0;x,t,s)\ \text{for some }A_0\text{ with }|A_0|\le\Lambda_0 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{A parametric-maximum fact.}
We first record that the maximum of a continuous function over a fixed nonempty
compact set is a continuous function of the remaining parameters. Let $P$ be a
metric space, let $Q$ be a nonempty compact metric space and let $H$ be
real-valued and continuous on $P\times Q$. For each $\pi\in P$ the function
$H(\pi,\cdot)$ is continuous on the nonempty compact $Q$, so
$\max_{q\in Q}H(\pi,q)$ exists (preamble). Let $\pi_k\to\pi$ in $P$. Testing
against a fixed $q\in Q$ gives $\max_{q'}H(\pi_k,q')\ge H(\pi_k,q)$, and
$H(\pi_k,q)\to H(\pi,q)$ by continuity of $H$, so
$\liminf_k\max_{q'}H(\pi_k,q')\ge H(\pi,q)$ for every $q\in Q$ and therefore
$\liminf_k\max_{q'}H(\pi_k,q')\ge\max_qH(\pi,q)$. Conversely choose for each $k$
a maximizer $q_k\in Q$ of $H(\pi_k,\cdot)$ and let a subsequence be given; by
compactness of $Q$ it has a further subsequence along which $q_k\to\bar q\in Q$
(Bolzano--Weierstrass, preamble). Along it $(\pi_k,q_k)\to(\pi,\bar q)$, so by
continuity of $H$,
\[
  \max_{q'}H(\pi_k,q')=H(\pi_k,q_k)\longrightarrow H(\pi,\bar q)
   \le\max_qH(\pi,q).
\]
Write $a_k=\max_{q'}H(\pi_k,q')$ and $L=\max_qH(\pi,q)$. Every subsequence of
$(a_k)$ thus has a further subsequence converging to a real number at most $L$;
by the first bound every subsequential limit of $(a_k)$ is also at least $L$, so
every subsequence of $(a_k)$ has a further subsequence converging to $L$ exactly.
In particular no subsequence of $(a_k)$ diverges, so $(a_k)$ is bounded; and a
bounded real sequence all of whose subsequences have a further subsequence
converging to $L$ converges to $L$, since otherwise some $\varepsilon>0$ would
admit infinitely many $k$ with $|a_k-L|\ge\varepsilon$, and that subsequence
would have no further subsequence converging to $L$. Hence $a_k\to L$. As $P$ is
a metric space, sequential continuity is continuity, so
$\pi\mapsto\max_{q\in Q}H(\pi,q)$ is continuous on $P$. Applying this to $-H$ and
using $\min_qH(\pi,q)=-\max_q(-H)(\pi,q)$, the map
$\pi\mapsto\min_{q\in Q}H(\pi,q)$ is continuous on $P$ as well.

\emph{Continuity of $\Gamma$.}
Put $P=\{A\in F:|A|\le\Lambda_0+1\}\times S$ and $Q=\{y\in F:|y|\le M\}$, each
with the metric induced from the ambient finite-dimensional space, and
\[
  H\bigl((A,x,t,s),y\bigr)=f(y,t)-\frac{|y|^2}{2s}-A\cdot(y-x).
\]
$Q$ is nonempty and compact, being a closed bounded subset of the
finite-dimensional space $F$ (preamble). $H$ is continuous on $P\times Q$:
the map $((A,x,t,s),y)\mapsto(y,t)$ is continuous and sends $P\times Q$ into
$\Omega$, because every triple of $S$ has $t\in I$, and $f$ is continuous on
$\Omega$; the map $((A,x,t,s),y)\mapsto|y|^2/(2s)$ is continuous on $P\times Q$
because $s>0$ on $S$, which is a compact set of triples with positive third
coordinate, so $s$ does not vanish there; and
$((A,x,t,s),y)\mapsto A\cdot(y-x)$ is continuous, the inner product being
continuous in both arguments. By hypothesis (iii),
$\Gamma(A;x,t,s)=\max_{y\in Q}H((A,x,t,s),y)$ for every $(A,x,t,s)\in P$ — the
supremum over $Q$ in (iii) being a maximum because $H((A,x,t,s),\cdot)$ is
continuous on the nonempty compact $Q$. So by the recorded fact
$(A,x,t,s)\mapsto\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$ is continuous on $P$, which is the first
assertion.

\emph{The infimum over all slopes is a minimum over a ball.}
Let $(x,t,s)\in S$. By hypothesis (i) the number $\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$ is a real
number for every $A\in F$, so the family $\{\Gamma(A;x,t,s):A\in F\}$ is a
nonempty set of reals, containing $\Gamma(0;x,t,s)$. Put
$Q_0=\{A\in F:|A|\le\Lambda_0\}$, a nonempty compact set containing $0$. By the
previous paragraph $A\mapsto\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$ is continuous on $Q_0$, which is
contained in $\{|A|\le\Lambda_0+1\}$, so it attains a minimum on $Q_0$ at some
$A_0$ with $|A_0|\le\Lambda_0$ (preamble). We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bgma-min}
  \Gamma(A_0;x,t,s)\le\Gamma(A;x,t,s)\qquad(A\in F).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $|A|\le\Lambda_0$ this is the minimality of $A_0$ on $Q_0$. If
$|A|>\Lambda_0$ then by hypothesis (ii), read in contrapositive,
$\Gamma(A;x,t,s)>\Gamma(0;x,t,s)+1>\Gamma(0;x,t,s)\ge\Gamma(A_0;x,t,s)$, the last
inequality being the minimality of $A_0$ on $Q_0$ applied at $0\in Q_0$. This is
\eqref{eq:bgma-min}.

\emph{The continuous minimal value.}
Apply the recorded parametric fact a second time, with $P=S$, $Q=Q_0$ and
$H((x,t,s),A)=\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$, in the minimum form: $Q_0$ is nonempty and
compact, and $H$ is continuous on $S\times Q_0$ because
$Q_0\times S\subseteq\{|A|\le\Lambda_0+1\}\times S$ and $\Gamma$ is continuous
there by the first assertion, the exchange of the two factors being a
homeomorphism. Hence
\[
  G(x,t,s):=\min_{|A|\le\Lambda_0}\Gamma(A;x,t,s)
\]
defines a function continuous on $S$; extend it to all of $F\times\R\times\R$ by
giving it the value $0$ off $S$, which does not affect continuity on $S$. For
$(x,t,s)\in S$ the minimum is attained at the $A_0$ produced above, so
$G(x,t,s)=\Gamma(A_0;x,t,s)$ with $|A_0|\le\Lambda_0$, and by
\eqref{eq:bgma-min} $G(x,t,s)\le\Gamma(A;x,t,s)$ for every $A\in F$. These are
the two displayed properties, and the proof is complete.
\end{proof}
