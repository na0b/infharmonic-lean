\begin{lemma}[Joint continuity of evaluating a varying form on a varying direction]
Let $Z$ be a topological space, let $E$ be a real normed space, let
$B:Z\to\mathcal L\bigl(E,\mathcal L(E,\R)\bigr)$ assign to each $w\in Z$ a continuous
bilinear form on $E$, and let $u:Z\to E$. If $B$ and $u$ are both continuous at
$z\in Z$, then so is
\[
  w\longmapsto B(w)\bigl[u(w),\,u(w)\bigr].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The evaluation map
\[
  \mathcal L(E,G)\times E\longrightarrow G,\qquad (C,x)\longmapsto C(x)
\]
is bilinear and bounded, since $\|C(x)\|\le\|C\|\,\|x\|$; hence it is continuous, for
every real normed space $G$.

\emph{Step 1.} Apply this with $G=\mathcal L(E,\R)$. The map $w\mapsto(B(w),u(w))$ is
continuous at $z$, because a map into a product is continuous at a point exactly when
both of its components are, and both $B$ and $u$ are continuous at $z$ by hypothesis.
Composing with the evaluation map, which is continuous at the point $(B(z),u(z))$, shows
that
\[
  w\longmapsto B(w)\bigl[u(w),\,\cdot\,\bigr]\in\mathcal L(E,\R)
\]
is continuous at $z$.

\emph{Step 2.} Apply the same evaluation map with $G=\R$. By Step 1 the map
$w\mapsto\bigl(B(w)[u(w),\cdot\,],\,u(w)\bigr)$ is continuous at $z$, again because each
component is. Composing with the evaluation map, continuous at the point
$\bigl(B(z)[u(z),\cdot\,],u(z)\bigr)$, gives the continuity at $z$ of
\[
  w\longmapsto B(w)\bigl[u(w),\,u(w)\bigr],
\]
which is the assertion.
\end{proof}
