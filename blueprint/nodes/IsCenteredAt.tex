\begin{definition}[Centered at a point]
Let $F$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, let $C_0\in\R$, let
$v$ be a real function on the space-time $\ell^2(F\times\R)$ and let $z_c$ be a
space-time point. We say that $v$ is \emph{centered at $z_c$ with constant
$C_0$} if
\[
  v(z_c)=0
\]
and $(0,0)$ is a fit for $v$ at $z_c$ of radius $1$ with constant $C_0$, in the
sense of \noderef{IsFit}; explicitly
\[
  |v(x_c+h,t_c+\ell)|\le C_0
  \qquad(|\ell|\le1,\ |h|\le1),
\]
where $z_c=(x_c,t_c)$.
\end{definition}
