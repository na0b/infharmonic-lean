\begin{definition}[Subquadratic spatial growth on compact time intervals]
Let $F$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, let $I\subseteq\R$ and
let $f$ be a real function on the space-time $\ell^2(F\times\R)$. We say $f$ has
\emph{subquadratic spatial growth on compact time intervals of $I$} if for every
compact $J\subseteq I$
\[
  \lim_{M\to\infty}\ \sup_{\substack{|x|\ge M\\ t\in J}}
      \frac{|f(x,t)|}{|x|^2}=0 .
\]
Explicitly: for every compact $J\subseteq I$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is
$M>0$ such that $|f(x,t)|\le\varepsilon|x|^2$ whenever $|x|\ge M$ and $t\in J$.
\end{definition}
